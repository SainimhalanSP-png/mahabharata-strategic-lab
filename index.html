The Context: Chakravyūha

We use one episode from the epic: the Chakravyūha formation on the thirteenth day of the Kurukshetra war.

  • What it is : A military formation of concentric rings of warriors. Popular retellings describe seven tiers, arranged to draw in and trap those who enter.
  • Why entering is difficult : Each ring has its own defenders and only a few openings, so a route that looks short from outside can be blocked or costly inside.
  • Incomplete information : Abhimanyu knew how to enter the formation but not how to leave it.
  • Coordination : The plan depended on other warriors following through the opening he made. When held back, he was alone.
  • Consequences of poor decisions : An entry with no exit plan and no support turned a bold move into a trap.

Day 13 in five steps

  1. Drona arranges the Chakravyūha.
  2. Abhimanyu agrees to open a way in, knowing the entry but not the exit.
  3. He breaks into the formation; those following him are held at the gate.
  4. Deep inside, he fights alone and is surrounded.
  5. He falls. The episode is remembered as a lesson on planning, information and support.

The contemporary problem

People often make decisions where information is incomplete, resources are limited, risks differ between choices, and several possible actions lead to different outcomes.

Existing approaches

  • Human intuition
  • Trial and error
  • Potentially inconsistent decisions

Our approach

  • Represent the situation as a computational model
  • Apply algorithms
  • Compare possible strategies
  • Measure the resulting outcome

What does the epic teach us?

Strategy : Planning must account for the structure of the situation.
Information : Decisions made with incomplete information can increase risk.
Coordination : Individual action may not be sufficient in complex environments.
Consequences : A locally attractive decision may produce a worse global outcome.

Identified Gap

Based on our review of the selected existing works, we identified comparatively less emphasis on presenting Mahabharata-inspired strategic situations as interactive computational decision problems where users can compare their decisions with algorithmic strategies.
